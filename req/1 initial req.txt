Clinic Management System:

A clinic management software is required to develop in node js as web application.

The application has two roles operator, and management role for OPD management and Liberatory, ECG, ECO, Ultra sound, Operation Theater slips and cash management.

[ADDED] Access Control: Each operator and management user must log in with their own username/password. Screens and actions are restricted by role, and every cash transaction/action should record which user performed it (audit trail), since operators handle cash directly.

[ADDED] Additional points identified during analysis (please confirm with customer):

1. Payment modes: document only mentions "cash management" — confirm if card, bank transfer, or online payments must also be supported, or if it is strictly cash-only.

2. Currency & locale: assumed Pakistani Rupees (PKR) and Pakistan-style CNIC, since CNIC is used for patient ID. Confirm currency, date format, and whether Urdu-language support is needed alongside English.

3. Printing hardware: confirm printer type for slips/receipts (thermal counter printer vs A4 printer) and supply the hospital's logo/letterhead image for the slip header.

4. Data backup & retention: patient medical/financial data is sensitive — confirm backup frequency (e.g. daily automated backup) and how long records must be retained.

5. Concurrency & deployment: confirm how many operator counters/computers will run simultaneously (Liberatory, X-Ray, ECG, ECO, Ultra Sound, OT, reception), whether the system runs on a local clinic network (LAN) or needs remote/cloud access, and whether a database technology preference exists (e.g. MySQL/PostgreSQL/MongoDB).

6. Multi-branch: confirm if this is for a single clinic location only, or if multiple branches must be supported under one system.

7. Scalability & caching: the system should be able to scale to more counters/patients (and future multi-branch) without a redesign, and should serve frequently-read data (test/price catalogs, doctor list, dashboard summary totals) from a cache instead of hitting the database every time, to keep counter operations fast under concurrent load. Cache must be invalidated immediately whenever Management edits a price/catalog/doctor, so operators never bill an old price. Patient lookup, OPD/token number generation, and payment recording always read/write live data (never cached), to avoid duplicate tokens or stale balances.
